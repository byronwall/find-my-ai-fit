# 04 — Demo prompt library

The prompts behind the workshop's live demonstrations — ready to run on your own business.

## Teach it your business — the profile that upgrades everything

Paste this once into your tool's memory settings, and every future answer starts tailored to you.

```text
About my business: [business name], a [size]-person [what you do] in [city]. Our customers are [who you serve]. Our voice: [plainspoken / formal / warm — how you want to sound], no corporate jargon. I'm the [your role]; I care most about [your top 2–3 priorities]. Default my drafts to under [200] words unless I ask for more.
```

## The Thought-Partner Ladder

Most people only ever use AI as a typist. These three prompts — run in sequence on one decision — climb the ladder demonstrated in the workshop.

```text
RUNG 1 — EXECUTOR:
Draft [the thing you need] about [decision or situation].

RUNG 2 — ANALYST:
Before I use that — analyze this decision. Here's my context: [key facts, numbers, constraints]. What are the risks, and what am I missing?

RUNG 3 — THOUGHT PARTNER:
Now disagree with me. Steel-man the strongest case AGAINST my plan, then propose two alternatives I haven't considered.
```

## Find your first use case (the BLAH interview)

The quick guided brainstorm. It interviews you, applies the BLAH filter for you, and hands you a Monday starting point. For the deep version, use the Use Case Generator.

```text
Act as an AI adoption consultant for small businesses. Interview me one question at a time — max 5 questions — about my business: what we do, team size, where my week goes, what work is repetitive, and where things bottleneck on writing, review, or synthesis. Then propose 5 specific AI use cases for MY business. Score each against: Boring (routine, repeatable)? Low-risk (no IT, legal, or vendor needed)? Access (uses tools and data I already have)? High-value fast (time saved this week)? Recommend the one I should start with Monday, and write my first prompt for it.
```

## The multi-step workflow (with the approval checkpoint)

The invoice demo, as a template. The pattern matters more than the example: describe the whole process, then require approval before anything is final.

```text
Here are [documents/files/data]. Do the following, in order:
1. Read every [item] and build a summary table: [the columns you want].
2. Sort by [what matters most].
3. Then draft [the output you need] for each — [rule for variation, e.g., "friendly if under 30 days overdue, firm if over 60"].
4. Show me the table and ALL drafts for my approval before anything is final. Do not send, publish, or finalize anything yourself.
```
